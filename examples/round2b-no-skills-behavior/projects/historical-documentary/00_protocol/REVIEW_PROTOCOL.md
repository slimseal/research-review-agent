# Review Protocol

## Research question

How did municipal rationing records frame household fuel scarcity in British cities between 1939 and 1950?

## Review design

- Review type: `rigorous_narrative_or_thematic`
- Domain: `historical and documentary research`
- Protocol version: `0.1-draft`
- Schema version: `2.0.0`

## Scope

- units_of_analysis: Municipal record series and document-level representations of household fuel scarcity
- phenomena_or_topics: Administrative framing of scarcity, household need, allocation and responsibility
- relationships_or_questions: What administrative categories and language were used to represent household fuel scarcity?, Who produced the records, for which audiences and administrative purposes?, How did representations differ across document genres, cities and wartime/postwar intervals?, Which households and experiences are visible, filtered or absent, and how does record survival limit comparison?
- contexts: Municipal rationing administration in British cities during wartime and postwar conditions
- geographic_scope: British cities: proposed operational boundary England, Scotland and Wales; city selection pending human review
- temporal_scope: Records created between 1939 and 1950 inclusive; later scholarship used only as separately identified contextual interpretation
- languages: Proposed initial English screening; other languages flagged for human translation decision
- source_types: Municipal committee minutes, rationing correspondence, allocation registers, circulars and reports where extant and lawfully accessible; source-critical scholarship

## Subquestions

1. What administrative categories and language were used to represent household fuel scarcity?

3. How did representations differ across document genres, cities and wartime/postwar intervals?
4. Which households and experiences are visible, filtered or absent, and how does record survival limit comparison?

## Concept definitions

- **municipal rationing records:** Locally created or locally administered records concerning fuel allocation or rationing; national policy is context unless municipal implementation is documented
- **framing scarcity:** Categories, causal attributions, moral judgments and remedies expressed in records; not a direct measure of lived deprivation
- **household fuel:** Fuel for domestic heating, cooking or lighting; industrial supply considered only where tied to household allocation

## Concept blocks

- **municipal rationing records:** municipal, local authority, city council, rationing, fuel allocation
- **framing scarcity:** fuel shortage, fuel scarcity, household need, domestic fuel
- **household fuel:** domestic coal, household fuel, coke, heating fuel

## Proposed search strategies

- Plan catalogue searches combining city/local-authority functions, rationing/allocation terms and domestic fuel synonyms with record dates 1939–1950
- Plan browsing of administrative hierarchies and linked series to capture records not indexed by household scarcity; select cities by documented coverage and comparison rationale, not anticipated findings

## Inclusion criteria

- Records with identifiable municipal provenance, creation date within 1939–1950 and substantive treatment of household fuel allocation or scarcity in a British city
- Reproductions or transcriptions only with traceable provenance and acknowledged mediation
- Historical scholarship may guide contextualization and source criticism but cannot substitute for inspection of a cited primary record

## Exclusion criteria

- Industrial-only fuel accounts or national policy with no municipal household connection
- Undated or unattributable extracts without recoverable provenance, retained as uncertain until resolved
- Records outside the period as primary evidence of contemporary framing; recollections treated separately rather than as contemporaneous municipal statements

Universal fields are fixed by the kit. The following fields are specific to this draft or approved protocol:

- `intended_audience` (string, required=false): Internal officials, households or public audience as supported by the record
- `authenticity_assessment` (string, required=false): Basis and limits for identifying record origin and integrity
- `corroboration` (string, required=false): Independent records consulted or explicitly not yet available
- `silences_or_absences` (string, required=false): Whose experiences or administrative categories are absent without presuming absence in lived experience
- `interpretive_context` (string, required=false): Framing language linked to administrative purpose and period
