# Outline and Section Briefs

Academic generation is explicit Codex/researcher work using `writing-context`; `writing-submit` validates and persists the authored result. There is no standalone automatic outline model.

Phase 13 requires current approved Protocol and reviewed Phase 8–12 records. Organize around the question and evidence: concepts, historical development, mechanisms, material interpretation, methodological differences or debates as appropriate. Do not impose chapters, PICO, or a shared controversy on unrelated papers.

Each section needs purpose, argument progression, Claim IDs, applicable Gap IDs and planned counterarguments. Outline-level coverage, duplication and gap-connection statements explain decisions. IDs and current review status are mechanically checked; argumentative coherence and completeness across research questions require human judgment.

Phase 14 briefs must exactly match their outline section's Claim/Gap set. They must retain supporting, contradictory and qualifying normalized links, known evidence IDs and exact planned citation identities. They state restrictions, methodological issues, relevant theories (including justified absence), unresolved questions and prohibited claims. Unknown, stale, rejected or formally unapproved Claims are rejected. Outline/brief human review is separate and version-bound.

An intentionally empty gap map is possible only through the upstream reviewed none-warranted assessment. Deferred gaps cannot be made complete by writing a gap-shaped conclusion.

Example request: “Create an argument-led outline from this project's approved Claim Map, then show me unresolved architectural choices.” Read the phase procedure, inspect context and sources, author JSON, submit, and stop for the human reviewer. See `docs/writing-tooling.md` for commands.
